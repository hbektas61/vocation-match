# Decisions

These decisions are owner-approved and must not be silently changed.

| ID | Date | Decision | Reason | Revisit when |

| D-001 | 2026-07-24 | Upcoming access uses self-declared hotel and stay dates; no reservation proof is collected. | The owner wants minimum friction and minimum sensitive data. | Fraud or hotel-hopping data shows material harm. |
| D-002 | 2026-07-24 | Here Now access requires only a recent foreground check within 500 meters of the selected hotel. | Proximity is sufficient for the intended room. | Abuse data justifies stronger controls. |
| D-003 | 2026-07-24 | A user can have exactly one active hotel at any moment. | This is the primary scope and abuse constraint. | Never silently; owner decision required. |
| D-004 | 2026-07-24 | Changing hotels immediately closes discovery access in the previous hotel. Existing matches/chats may remain. | Preserve the one-hotel rule without destroying social connections. | User safety evidence suggests chat expiry. |
| D-005 | 2026-07-24 | Exact coordinates and exact live distances are never shown to users or placed in analytics. | Privacy and stalking-risk reduction. | Never loosen without explicit privacy review. |
| D-006 | 2026-07-24 | Payments and premium access are deferred to the next product phase. | Validate the core loop before monetization. | Core pilot flow is working and measured. |
| D-007 | 2026-07-24 | Product copy must say “self-declared upcoming” and “near the hotel now,” not “verified reservation/guest.” | Prevent misleading trust claims. | A real hotel or reservation integration exists. |
| D-008 | 2026-07-24 | MVP is 18+ and includes block/report from the first usable build. | Social matching requires baseline safety. | Never remove. |
| D-009 | 2026-07-25 | Claude Studio may integrate verified increments into `main` and push `origin/main` directly without asking or opening a PR. Temporary local worktree branches are allowed for isolation. | The owner wants continuous delivery without routine pull requests. | Owner explicitly revokes authorization. |
| D-010 | 2026-07-25 | The autonomous build runs four consecutive phases and pauses only after the complete MVP systems program, not after an intermediate milestone. | The owner wants three to four phases of uninterrupted implementation. | Owner explicitly changes the program boundary. |
| D-011 | 2026-07-25 | Blocking is reversible: `unblock_user()` plus a blocked-list in Settings. Unblocking does not restore the match the block ended. | Backlog R-002 flagged that an irreversible block is a UX defect an accidental tap cannot undo. Reversibility is the standard, safe default; the one-way part (no match resurrection) keeps the safety guarantee. | Abuse data shows unblocking is used to harass. |
| D-012 | 2026-07-25 | One swipe decision per pair, stored permanently. A repeat swipe is a no-op that returns the existing outcome rather than an error. | Idempotency is what makes the endpoint safe to retry over a flaky mobile connection, and it removes "swipe again to change your mind" from the MVP surface. | Users ask for an undo, or a rewind becomes a premium feature. |
| D-015 | 2026-07-25 | The on-device test pass is deferred to the next phase as an accepted, recorded risk. The MVP systems program closes without it. | No simulator or device toolchain exists on the build machine, and installing one is owner work. Everything a bundle can prove is proven; keeping the program open changed nothing. | The owner installs Xcode / the Android SDK, or connects a phone with Expo Go. Do this before any pilot with real users, not before the next code milestone. |
| D-014 | 2026-07-25 | Profile photos will be served from our own storage rather than an arbitrary URL. Until that exists, `photo_url` is capped at 2048 characters and must be https. | A card is shown in discovery without any interaction, so a self-hosted image URL is a passive beacon: the profile owner learns the IP and timing of everyone who merely saw them. On an app whose whole promise is not revealing who is near whom, that is the wrong default. Raised by the security audit. | The storage bucket exists and the client stops accepting arbitrary URLs. |
| D-013 | 2026-07-25 | Moderation escalates automatically at three distinct reporters (a FLAGGED action), and an actioned report suspends the account, which removes it from every room and stops it messaging. | The MVP needs a safety response that does not depend on someone watching a queue in real time. | Volume makes the threshold noisy in either direction. |

| D-016 | 2026-07-25 | A repeat swipe, and a photo read, never depend on where the other person is at that moment. A decision already made is answered from storage; a photo stays visible to someone who was already shown the card, whether or not its owner is checked in right now. | The pilot-hardening audit found that both endpoints answered from the target's *live* room eligibility, and a user id is public to everyone who has seen a card. Polling either one told you the moment a specific person arrived near the hotel and the moment they left — a live presence feed on someone who, in the swipe case, you had already been removed from the deck of. That is the behaviour D-005 exists to prevent, and it also broke D-012: a retry after a dropped response failed if the other person had moved in between. | Never loosen without a privacy review. Note what this does not close: the fast path does measurably less work than a first swipe, so a precise enough timing measurement could still tell the two apart. That is a far weaker signal than the one it replaced — it says whether you have swiped on someone, not where they are — and closing it would mean making every call do the slow path's work. Worth knowing before this ever faces a more adversarial setting than an invited pilot. |

| D-017 | 2026-07-26 | The way into the app is one onboarding wizard whose current step is *derived* from real state — session, profile, active hotel — rather than stored anywhere. Only the "show the teaching cards" flag lives in memory, and a cold start begins with it false. A profile without an active hotel resumes conservatively at bio: repeating optional steps is preferable to silently dropping them. | Storing a cursor means two sources of truth about where somebody is, and the one on the device is the one that can be wrong. Deriving it also gives the requirement "a finished onboarding must not reappear" for free, with nothing written to disk. | A step is ever added that the server has no way to observe. |
| D-018 | 2026-07-26 | `profiles.interests` exists: up to five self-chosen entries of 1–24 characters, readable on a discovery card and never used as a filter. Omitting the field from a profile write means "leave it alone", not "clear it". | The domain model has carried `interests` since the first milestone with nothing behind it, and the onboarding step that asks for them would otherwise throw the answer away. Bounded because the column is free text, and an unbounded array of unbounded strings is a place to put a payload. Not searchable, because something to read on a card is not the same as a facet to be targeted with. | Interests are ever wanted as a matching signal — that is a different privacy question. |
| D-019 | 2026-07-26 | Account entry is phone-only SMS OTP. The same six-digit code flow creates or restores an account; email/password UI and email sign-up are removed. Phone numbers stay in Supabase Auth and are never copied into profiles, discovery, analytics, or logs. | The owner expects people to use the app while travelling and explicitly chose the lower-friction phone path. One flow also avoids account enumeration and removes password handling from the app. | A reviewed identity migration or an additional sign-in method is explicitly approved. |

| D-020 | 2026-07-26 | The palette is one brand colour, `#E1C4FF`, on a white ground. Because that colour measures 1.55:1 against white it is never a control edge, never text on white, and never the only carrier of a state — it always appears with a darker sibling of the same hue (`#7B4FA8`, 5.96:1), with ink on top of it (11.68:1), or with a word or mark beside it. | The owner specified the hex and specified white surfaces. Both are honoured exactly; what could not be honoured is the implicit assumption that a colour this light can do a boundary's job, so the palette is arranged so that it never has to. | The brand colour changes to one that clears 3:1 on white, at which point the companion values can be simplified away. |
| D-021 | 2026-07-26 | The focused input border is exactly `#E1C4FF`, and the border alone changes: its weight goes from 1.5 to 2.5. **Amended same day by the owner**, who saw the filled version in the app and asked for the fill to go — the earlier form of this decision added a lavender ground and a `#7B4FA8` outer ring. | The owner asked for that precise border colour, and then for it to be the whole signal. The weight change is what keeps the state perceivable for someone the hue does not register for, so the state is still not colour-alone. | Never weaken to colour alone — the weight change is the floor. |
| D-022 | 2026-07-26 | Turkish phone entry is a fixed, non-editable `+90` beside the box; the editable value is ten national digits and E.164 is produced once, at the API call. Turkey-only parsing lives in `phoneTr.ts`, separate from the generic E.164 helpers in `phone.ts`. | A prefix that lives in the value can be half-deleted, and a number with a mangled country code fails at the provider rather than in the field. Keeping the strict, single-country parser separate lets the onboarding field reject a landline without narrowing what the rest of the app is able to accept. | Another country is supported, which needs a country picker rather than a second hardcoded prefix. |
| D-023 | 2026-07-26 | `show_me` filters discovery in **both** directions: A sees B only when A's answer matches B's gender and B's answer matches A's. WOMEN and MEN are the only two values the filter knows, so a person whose gender is neither is reachable only by someone asking to see everyone. Sexual orientation is never a filter — it is shown when its owner publishes it, and nothing more. | Filtering one way lets one person's preference override the other's. The two-value limit is a real exclusion and is written down rather than hidden: the alternative was folding every other gender into one of two buckets, which is worse, and guessing is not available. Orientation stays out of matching because inferring who somebody wants to meet from how they describe themselves is exactly the assumption this product should not make. | Reconsider when there is a way to let people outside the two say who should be able to find them, rather than the app deciding for them. Any change needs a privacy review. |
| D-024 | 2026-07-26 | A profile is finished when the server says so: `onboarding_completed_at`, set only by `complete_onboarding()`, which requires a gender and a show-me answer. The column is absent from every client grant. A profile without the mark is a draft and is excluded from discovery. Gender and show-me are required; orientation, passions and a photo are not. | "Has a profile row" stopped meaning "finished" the moment the profile was built across several screens — a row exists from the birthdate step onward, and somebody who closed the app on the gender question would otherwise have been a complete, discoverable profile with no say in who saw them. Photo stays optional because a required photo is a barrier to the people most careful about being seen, and the brief asked for the decision to be recorded either way. | A required answer is added, which means both a migration and a completion rule change. |
| D-025 | 2026-07-26 | Choosing a hotel is not part of finishing onboarding. A complete profile enters the app with no active hotel; the hotel is asked for when somebody reaches for something that needs one. | The hotel is a fact about right now and changes often, while the profile is made once. Blocking entry on it also meant a returning user who had left their hotel was thrown back into onboarding. | The product ever has content that is meaningless without a hotel on the first screen. |
| D-026 | 2026-07-26 | A profile holds up to nine ordered photos in `profile_photos`; `profiles.photo_path` stays derived from slot 1. **Amended same day by the owner**: a discovery card now carries the whole ordered set (`photo_paths`), because one photo on a card is not believable — which means the photo count and paths are visible to exactly the people who can see the card. The storage read policy needed no change: `app.may_view_photo` always authorised per owner, not per path, so sharing names with the people the policy already admits is the policy working. The set's management surface (add/remove/reorder) remains owner-only. | The original owner-only rule protected the count from the room; the owner weighed that against a one-photo card reading as fake and chose believability, explicitly. | The card must never carry photos of somebody the viewer could not see through `may_view_photo` — the feed's own eligibility filters are what guarantee that today. |
| D-027 | 2026-07-26 | Photos are reordered by hold-and-drag; the caption says so and is now true. **Amended same day by the owner**, who used the arrow-button version and asked for the photos back — no arrows, no overlay band on the pictures. The assistive path survives invisibly: each tile carries "move earlier/later" accessibility actions, which is how a screen reader reorders since a drag target is not something VoiceOver can hand you. The hold-before-lift (180 ms) is what keeps a scroll that begins on a photo being a scroll. | The first version chose visible controls precisely because a described-but-absent gesture is a caption for a different app; once the owner asked for the gesture, the honest move was to build it and keep the assistive equivalent, not to argue for the buttons. | The accessibility actions are the floor — the drag must never become the only way to reorder. |
| D-028 | 2026-07-26 | Local files are read with `expo-file-system`'s `File`, never `fetch('file://…')`. | `fetch` reaches the network stack: on Android that is OkHttp, which has no handler for the `file` scheme, so every upload failed there. On iOS it succeeds and then `arrayBuffer()` routes through React Native's `FileReader`, which asks the native side for a base64 data URL and decodes it in JS — an extra full copy of a multi-megabyte image, in string form, on the JS thread. The dependency is added at the version `expo` pins for SDK 54; it is not an SDK change. | Never reintroduce `fetch` on a `file://` URI. |
| D-029 | 2026-07-26 | The hotel catalogue is materialised lazily from OpenStreetMap. The `hotel-search` edge function searches the local table first; when it answers thinly it asks Nominatim (Turkey-only, hotel-type places), writes the hits through `upsert_hotel_from_provider('osm', …)`, and searches again. The client falls back to the catalogue-only RPC when the function is unreachable. The hotel screen carries the ODbL attribution line. | The owner is right that hand-seeding every hotel does not scale — and the schema always said the table was a provider-fed cache; the seed file was just the only provider it ever had. OSM over a commercial places API because of the licence: ODbL permits storing the data with attribution, while Google's terms forbid caching place data beyond an ID — incompatible with a product whose design is "activate a hotel that lives in our table". The table grows only with what people actually search, bounded by the real world, not by traffic. | A commercial provider is licensed, or Nominatim's 1-request-per-second policy stops fitting the traffic — that is the point to put a queue or a paid geocoder in the function. |
| D-030 | 2026-07-26 | The app speaks English and Turkish. The choice is offered on the first screen (each option labelled in its own language), changeable later in Settings, and persisted on the device. `COPY` is a live module binding switched by `setLocale`; English defines the shape and Turkish must match it key for key, so a sentence cannot ship in one language only. The D-007 trust-copy audit runs over both languages, with Turkish stem-matching, because a promise that holds in English and breaks in Turkish is broken. | The pilot is Turkish; the codebase was English-only. A proxy over the copy object was rejected on a real ground: `Object.entries` cannot see through a proxy with an empty target, and that is exactly how the trust audit walks the sentences — the mechanism must be visible to its own auditor. | A third language arrives — the pattern extends by one file that the compiler forces to completeness. |
| D-031 | 2026-07-26 | Push notifications exist, in exactly two kinds. MESSAGE: the sender's name and a fixed sentence — never the message body, because lock screens have readers. ROOM_NEW: "somebody new at your hotel", nameless, sent to the room-eligible people at that hotel, at most once per person per hotel per six hours. Triggers queue transactionally in SQL (pgTAP-proven); pg_cron + pg_net drain the queue to Expo's push API every minute. Tokens are device credentials: owner-only rows, registered with the device's language after onboarding completes, re-registered on language change, removed before sign-out. No token → no queue row. No client can read the queue. | The owner asked for message and room-arrival notifications. The privacy lines are the product's own: message content and arrival identity are for inside the app, where every existing rule (blocks, eligibility, visibility toggles) already governs them — a push is a knock on the door, not the letter itself. | Adding a notification kind, changing the dedupe window, or putting any personal content in a push body — each is a decision, not a tweak. |
| D-032 | 2026-07-26 | The hotel card tells you how many people are in each room — but only when the crowd hides the individual. `hotel_room_counts` returns the exact number of other eligible people at five or more, and null below; null renders as nothing at all, never "a few" or "somebody", because at one person even "somebody is here" is a presence leak. The count ignores show_me in both directions (a room's population is a fact about the room), never includes the caller, drops suspended and incomplete accounts, and is per active hotel only. Owner intent, recorded not built: this signal becomes a premium perk when the premium phase opens; until decisions.md advances that phase there is no entitlement, no billing, and the count is visible to every account. | The owner asked for the count with a threshold ("eşikle göster") and named premium as its eventual home. Five is the smallest crowd in which no one is anyone. | Changing the threshold, adding any below-threshold wording, or gating the count behind premium — each reopens this decision. |

| D-035 | 2026-07-27 | The Upcoming room is scoped to the caller's own declared dates: the deck shows only people whose stay crosses yours, inclusive at the edges (a checkout day and a checkin day are one shared day). | D-036 | 2026-07-28 | **Premium entitlement exists; billing still does not.** The rules, all server-enforced: a free member in the Upcoming room gets 3 likes and 5 passes, counted per hotel from stored swipes (a new hotel starts a fresh allowance; a replayed swipe never spends one); the Here Now room is Premium-only — the premium test sits inside `app.room_eligible` itself, so feed, headcount, my_rooms and swipe-target checks agree in the same instant, and `record_presence_check` refuses a free member before ever taking a location. | D-037 | 2026-07-28 | **The anchor place is a vacation venue, not only lodging — and the search forgives real typing.** The provider filter admits OSM `bar`, `nightclub`, `beach_resort` and named public beaches (`natural=beach`, same day: "beachleri kesin almalıyız") alongside hotel types, because the summer crowd gathers at beach clubs (the owner's case: Before Sunset, Alaçatı, tagged `amenity=bar`); the filter's job stays "named venues in, bare geography out". `search_hotels` adds squashed matching (joined-up words) and token matching (at most one stray word in a multi-word query); Nominatim gets one last-word-dropped retry when empty-handed. | Owner: "Alaçatı'daki Before Sunset Beach çıkmıyor, oralarda çok yoğunluk var" — and the observed misses "beforesunset" / "before sunset beach" on a row the catalogue already held. | Admitting broader amenity types (restaurants, cafés), any fuzzier matching, or a commercial places API as the discovery source. |

## Open owner decisions

- **Should the Upcoming room require overlapping stay dates?** Today it does
  not: anyone with a stay at that hotel that has not ended yet is in the room,
  so two people staying six months apart can match. Filtering by overlap is
  what most people would expect, but it shrinks an already thin pilot pool,
  and pool density is the thing most likely to make the first hotel feel dead.
  Left broad on purpose; one `where` clause changes it. (Resolved 2026-07-27: D-035 added that clause.)
- **Suspension is per account, not per person.** A suspended user can sign up
  again with another phone number — and, since H2, can delete the suspended
  account first, so nothing is even left behind. Closing that needs device or
  stronger identity signals, which is a bigger privacy decision than the MVP
  should make on its own. Worth sizing honestly before the pilot.
- **A coordinated pile-on can force a moderation flag.** Three distinct
  reporters raise a `FLAGGED` row automatically (D-013), and three disposable
  addresses are not hard to get. What it cannot do is suspend anyone —
  `resolve_report` is service_role only, so a human still stands between the
  flag and any consequence. Disposable phone numbers cost more than disposable
  email addresses but do not eliminate this risk. The flag is queue priority,
  not a ban. Whoever
  watches the queue during the pilot should know it is a lever.
- **Fake profiles: proposed and advised against (2026-07-28).** The owner
  floated seeding ~10 fake women's profiles into thin Upcoming rooms so the
  app never looks empty before a premium purchase. Studio assessment, given
  in full in chat: this is the FTC-v-Match / Ashley Madison fact pattern —
  deceptive commercial practice (no force required, influencing the purchase
  decision suffices), store-policy ban territory, KVKK/personality-rights
  exposure on the photos, and commercially self-defeating (likes into a void
  → refunds and one-star reviews; the hotel setting makes the fabrication
  physically checkable). Recommended instead, awaiting owner go: premium
  sales stay closed below a room-density threshold ("founding member" free
  premium until then) and the empty room becomes an invite mechanic. Fake
  profiles are not built and should not be.
- First pilot city and hotels.
- Whether existing chats expire after the trip.
- Premium package and price in the next phase. (2026-07-28: the entitlement
  and its rules exist — D-036 — but purchasing does not; price, package and
  store billing remain open.)
- **Premium direct message (D-036 follow-up).** The owner's rule includes
  "premiumsa doğrudan mesaj atabilir" — chat without a mutual match. That
  needs its own model (a pre-match conversation the other person can refuse),
  safety rules, and screens; staged as the next premium slice, not smuggled
  into this one.
